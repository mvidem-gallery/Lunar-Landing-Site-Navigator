METHOD & LIMITATIONS

Real data.
Honest resolution.

Terrain uses NASA LRO / LOLA elevation data. Between 59.3°S and 59.3°N, the nearest ±18 km uses SLDEM2015 (LOLA + Kaguya stereo terrain) at approximately 60 m grid spacing when available. This fills gaps between laser tracks, so it can contain more real detail than the 30 m LOLA-only grid. The metadata panel reports the active observer DEM. Grid spacing is not measurement accuracy: interpolation cannot recover unmeasured rocks or small craters. No synthetic craters are added.

A bundled 20 m polar DEM covers a 40 km square around the south pole. Outside stereo coverage, or if stereo data cannot be loaded, the nearest area uses LDEM_1024 (about 30 m north–south spacing) when available, except within 0.3° of a pole. Outer layers use 118 m, 474 m and approximately 3.8 km grids. Missing online data falls back to the bundled maps.

The 3D surface follows lunar curvature and extends about 490 km in each horizontal direction, beyond the visible horizon at the maximum 10 km camera lift. Concentric grids decrease detail with distance, with matched boundaries and surface-derived shading normals. Terrain height is shown at 1:1 scale; inspection lighting does not alter calculations.

The Orbital imagery switch overlays NASA Moon Trek / LRO WAC photographs (about 100 m source pixels). These show fixed acquisition lighting and shadows, not the selected mission time. Turn imagery off to view geometric shading alone. Photographs add appearance detail; they do not change elevation, horizon or mission calculations. Nearby images blend into a wider photo mosaic instead of a flat gray border. Above 75° latitude, polar stereographic images replace the equatorial mosaic to avoid longitude seams. Transparent nodata uses the wider layer. If imagery cannot be loaded, the view falls back to untextured terrain. Polar imagery tiles are served at about 268 m per pixel; underlying elevation remains independently resolved.

Sun and Earth positions, distances and angular diameters come from NASA / JPL Horizons, using a lunar observer and MOON_ME coordinates, with no atmospheric refraction. True scale uses JPL angular diameters. The Discs control magnifies the displayed diameter threefold without changing the calculations. The Sun's glow is a visual effect; Earth uses NASA Blue Marble with the JPL sub-observer and sub-solar coordinates. Its texture is a historical mosaic. The stars are from HYG v3.8, aligned with the lunar sky using the IAU orientation model.

The mission horizon includes lunar curvature out to 250 km at 0.5° azimuth spacing. Visibility uses the center of each disc. Partial visibility, eclipses and radio link budgets are not simulated. Camera lift changes only the view; calculations use the observer height from the form.

Incidence is measured from the local slope normal: 0° is perpendicular and 90° is grazing. Signal delay is geometric distance divided by light speed, excluding equipment and network delays.

Durations and event dates are interpolated estimates; short events can be missed between samples. Darkness endurance assumes a charged battery at landing and resets whenever direct sunlight returns. The lander is illustrative and aligned with the local terrain normal. This is a research prototype, not a validated flight planning tool.
